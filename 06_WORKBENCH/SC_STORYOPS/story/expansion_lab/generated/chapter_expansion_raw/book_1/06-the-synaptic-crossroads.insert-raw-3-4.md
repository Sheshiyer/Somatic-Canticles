Corv exhales through his teeth, a thin, almost inaudible whistle that makes the sphere’s silver seam flicker like an old filament. “Fracture’s too polite,” he says. “It’s waiting for us to apologise first.”  

Sona lifts an eyebrow. “You want etiquette from a topological scar?”  

“Wouldn’t be the strangest thing I’ve dated,” Corv replies, then catches the word *dated* too late, winces, and turns the wince into a shrug. The shrug costs him; the tether bites his clavicle and the obsidian shard clicks against his molars like a loose tooth.  

Jian glances up from the cuff, half-smile already forming, then thinks better of it. “Ethics note,” he says instead, fingers hovering above the dials. “If we cross while the sphere is still re-indexing, the archive will tag this as non-consensual transit. That sticks to the cortical watermark—could gum up future debriefs.”  

Gideon snorts. “Future debriefs,” he mutters. “Optimist.” He pockets the cracked stylus he’s been worrying between knuckles. “Consent is retroactive in here anyway. The corridor only remembers what we decide it remembered.”  

Sona rolls her wrist, letting the tether glide along her pulse. “Retroactive, sure. But the body keeps its own ledger.” She tilts the sphere so the violet light paints a bruise-coloured halo on the wall behind them. “Mine’s already scribbling margin notes about adrenaline debt and unpaid REM cycles.”  

Corv leans in, lowers his voice so only she can hear. “Give me the bruise-coloured halo as collateral. I’ll pay it back with interest once we’re topside.”  

She almost laughs—then doesn’t. The laugh would spill the quiet they’ve been nursing since the elevator forgot itself. Instead she offers the smallest nod, an IOU signed in pupil dilation.  

Gideon clears his throat like a man remembering he’s still breathing. “Pressure check,” he says. “Everyone’s endocrine muse still singing alto or have we gone full soprano?”  

“Mine’s beatboxing,” Jian answers, surprised by his own honesty. The cuff chirps once, a single bored metronome click, as if to corroborate.  

Corv taps the obsidian shard against the sphere’s vacant cradle; the sound is a soft clack, lighter than a knuckle on wood, heavier than silence. “We wait for the line to steady,” he says, “or we walk on a wobble and trust our ankles to remember geometry that hasn’t been invented yet.”  

Sona watches the wavering projection on the threshold. The silver seam shivers, then steadies, then shivers again—like a decision breathing. “Two-point-seven,” she amends, the number precise by instinct.  

Gideon’s lips twitch. “Rounding up,” he reminds them, but softer now, as if rounding were a kindness.
