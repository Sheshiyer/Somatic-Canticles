The officer’s jaw twitched. “And dignity buys us what?”  
“Time to pretend we’re merciful,” Sona answered, eyes on the cradle. She had already begun the slow withdrawal of her field from the lattice, letting the residual echo cool inside her own ribs. Thirty more seconds and the weave would collapse into polite compliance. She felt Corv’s attention like a pressure gauge behind her shoulder, noting the minute dilation of her pupils as she judged the last hinge.

A low chirp from the drone overhead—regulation acknowledging the buffer flush. Sona exhaled once, deliberate, tasting the cardamom trace that had lodged itself between her molars. The officer leaned closer, voice pitched below the drone’s mic threshold.  
“You ever wonder if the host feels grateful?”  
“She feels finished,” Sona said. “Gratitude is above my pay grade.”  
His laugh was a single, startled note, half-cough, half-surrender. “You’re brutal.”  
“I’m accurate,” she corrected. “Brutal was the part where we cracked her ribs six hours ago.” The words left no sting; they were simply the currency here.

The cradle clicked as the internal seal pressurized. Sona flexed her left hand, feeling the tremor migrate from metacarpal to wrist—an aftershock of sympathetic coupling, twelve percent and falling. She let it ride. The officer keyed the final lock, but his gaze lingered on the matte surface now opaque behind its mother-of-pearl sheen.  
“You’ll need to log the extraction sequence manually,” he said. “Drone’s buffer already erased local cache—Halden’s orders.”  
“Already logged,” Jian called from the corridor mouth, stylus flicking across his slate. “Redundant node, triple-wrapped. They can subpoena the air for all I care.”

Sona stepped back, boots silent on the anti-static grid. The corridor outside smelled of ozone and the faintest ghost of cedar used to mask sterility. Overhead the colour temperature had inched warmer by two hundred kelvin, as if the building itself exhaled with borrowed ease. She tasted copper again—her own blood this time, not the host’s—where the inside of her cheek had worried itself against a molar throughout the weave. Good. Real tissue, not borrowed resonance.

Corv touched her elbow, a gesture so light it might have been static electricity. “We carry forward what refuses to be erased,” he murmured. “That’s the contract.”  
Gideon was already ahead, palm flat against the service-conduit hatch. “Clock’s running—twelve minutes before oversight re-asserts. Let’s not give Halden a clean shot.”

They moved single file down the narrow ladder shaft. The air grew hotter with each rung—welding resin, ozone, the mineral tang of fresh alloys. Sona counted silently: eighteen, twenty, twenty-three, matching the cadence to her own newly widened exhale. At the last rung she paused, listening for any echo of the host’s rhythm inside her pulse. Silence. Clean, unremarkable silence. The ghost had filed its final return and closed the ledger.

Behind her the officer climbed last, toothpick still clamped in the corner of his mouth like a makeshift fuse. When he reached the top he glanced back once more toward the sealed chamber far below, then tucked the toothpick into his breast pocket as if it were evidence rather than habit. Sona met his eyes across the dim, and for the first time he looked not like protocol but like a man who had just seen his own mother exhale without fear.
