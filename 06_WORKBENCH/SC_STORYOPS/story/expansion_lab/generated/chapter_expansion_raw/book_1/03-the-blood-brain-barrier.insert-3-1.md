“Before we leave, one of us has to sign the fuck-up sheet,” Jian says, holding the canister like a lantern. Its sterile air purrs, a tabby that knows it will never be fed.

Corv raises an eyebrow. “I thought the membrane already countersigned in capillary braille.”

“That’s a souvenir,” Jian corrects. “The sheet is for the living. Ethics wants a thumb, not a fold of cerebellum origami.”

Gideon, who has been counting the seconds since the silver cooled, produces a black gel cap from his coat pocket. He flips it once, twice. “Thumbprint’s a misnomer while we’re still wearing sub-perineural gloves. Capillary walls can’t read ridges.”

Sona, still humming the unreleased seventh, frowns at the elevator console. “Console’s locked to biometric hash. If we don’t feed it a quorum signature, the shaft drops us straight into the spinal fluid reclamation sump for decontamination. Twenty minutes of cold phosphate rinse.”

Corv smiles—the small one that never reaches his eyes. “You’re saying we could surface as vertebrate soup.”

“Polite soup,” Jian mutters. “With documentation.”

The four of them stand in a loose square, the violet membrane inside Corv’s boot pressing against tendon like a second heartbeat. Gideon breaks the standoff first, pressing the gel cap against the star-shaped scar on his forearm. The capsule cracks, releasing a bead of his own cooled silver solder. The metal beads, spins, then settles into the shape of a miniature key.

He offers it to Sona. “Technically, the note you’re humming is a C-sharp diminished. That’s the harmonic key the console was built to forgive. You sign, it unlocks, we all keep our cytoskeletons.”

Sona looks at the others. Consent, once a philosophical luxury inside the barrier, is now a mechanical switch.

“I’m not the one who folded a lobe like laundry,” she says, but she takes the key anyway.

Jian exhales, a soft valve hiss. “We’ll list the membrane as salvageable tissue, logged under Crowley protocol. No names, just four anonymous chords in need of resolution.”

Corv nods. “Better unresolved than dissected.”

Sona presses the silver key to the console; the elevator hums the note she’s been withholding. It is not forgiveness, exactly, but it is the closest thing the shaft can offer.

Quietly, the platform begins to rise.
