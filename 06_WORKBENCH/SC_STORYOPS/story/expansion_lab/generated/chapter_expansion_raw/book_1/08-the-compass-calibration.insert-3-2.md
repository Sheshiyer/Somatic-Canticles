“Four hundred grams,” Gideon muttered, patting the journal pocket. “Weighed against our margin for error, that’s an entire metabolic day.”  
Sona snorted. “Panic’s low-density. You’ll burn it before dawn.”  
Jian angled his wrist so the lattice ghost of the bearing glowed between them. “Checks out. Panic’s mostly nitrogen and adrenaline. Efficient fuel—if you let it oxidise.”  
Their laughter was small, dry, the kind that keeps teeth from chattering.

The corridor widened a handspan; humidity condensed on Sona’s upper lip, tasting of rust and printer ink.  
She wiped it with the back of her glove, then flicked the bead toward Jian.  
He caught it reflexively, examined the tiny red smear. “Signature matches the hemoglobin tracer. We’re still inside our own capillary map.”  
“Good,” she said. “I hate borrowing circulation.”

Gideon fished the journal halfway out, thumb hovering. “Protocol says we inventory contents. No exceptions.”  
Sona laid two fingers on the cracked spine. “Consent first. Failure gets a vote even when absent.”  
Jian exhaled. “Let’s ask the thing, then.” He angled his microphone cuff toward the pocket. “Candidate, do you object to post-mortem indexing?”  
Silence. The corridor lights flickered once, like a shrug.  
Gideon slid it back in. “Abstention noted.”

They walked three more paces before the pressure valve ticked.  
A bead of coolant hissed from Jian’s lattice cuff, forming a perfect silver sphere. It drifted upward, suctioned to the ceiling, then burst—sound of distant applause.  
Sona laughed again, louder this time. “System relief. The bearing’s no longer pressurised in silicon; it’s equalising through us.”  
“Biology wins the staring contest,” Gideon said. “Always does.”

Ahead, the fork’s two mouths waited. Diesel and night air curled from the right; left exhaled something colder, mineral, like the inside of a glacier.  
Sona inhaled both drafts, one nostril each, then exhaled through her teeth to mix them.  
“Decision?” Jian asked.  
She tapped her sternum twice—old bus-fare habit. “Memory already cast the vote. Consensus is just choreography.”  
Gideon adjusted the journal’s weight so it rode closer to his ribs, keeping step with her.
