Corv’s slate pings: *bio-feed integrity ninety-four percent*. He thumbs the alert silent, eyes still on Jian’s jugular where the collar’s green strobe hunts for a pulse.  
“Your adrenals are singing baritone,” he tells Jian. “Want me to damp them or let the chorus finish the aria?”

Jian answers by lifting his gloved hand, two fingers raised—field shorthand for *hold*. The tremor in those fingers is microscopic; Sona’s console flags it anyway, labels it *artifact or intention unclear*. She frowns, toggles the filter from *artifact* to *intention*, watches the line smooth.

“Correction,” she murmurs, not to them but to the log. “Operator micro-tremor resolved by placebo filter. Note: belief is cheaper than chemistry.”

Corv’s mouth quirks. “Cheaper until the audit.”

“Audit buys the placebo too,” Sona says. “Toth wrote that in the margin. Crowley crossed it out, then underlined it. We obey the underlining.”

The corridor lights dim to baseline maintenance, a slow fade that feels like a mother counting to ten before she speaks. Coolant fans throttle down; the air turns from machined mint to something closer to human breath.

Jian finally lowers his hand. “Status?”

“Compass jitter at 0.17,” Corv says. “Down from 0.42. Your cortisol’s backing off the cliff. You can stop pretending the roof is falling.”

“I wasn’t pretending,” Jian answers. “I was stress-testing the roof.”

Sona snorts. “Next time put in a ticket first. Maintenance union gets cranky about unscheduled apocalypses.”

The soft click of her releasing the safety is the only applause the joke earns. Silence pools, not ominous but ordinary, like the pause after tuning an instrument and before the first note decides it’s brave enough.

Corv pockets the chronograph, flexes the wrist that still remembers yesterday’s sprain. “We ride the new gain for three minutes. If the drift stays below 0.2, we call the calibration clean and walk. Objections?”

Jian looks at the droplet clinging to his cuff, then at Sona. “Only the usual: that ninety-eight percent mercy still leaves two percent of me un-mercied.”

Sona meets his gaze, deadpan. “File a ticket.”
