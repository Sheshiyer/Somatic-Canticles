A hush settled, not silence but the kind of pause the body grants when the heart forgets which beat comes next. The cardamom fug still clung to the membranes, thin as memory yet sharp enough to cut the liturgy’s cadence. Sona flexed her fingers; the last flakes of green had dissolved into the sweat of her palm, leaving only the taste of kitchens and the faint, electric prickle of an eight-residue fragment humming at 2.7 hertz against her pulse.

Corv watched the readout on her bead, pupils narrowed to pinpricks. “We’ve got a lag,” he murmured. “Cortisol rising, but not in lockstep. She’s dragging the beat.”

Jian’s stylus never left the scanner glass. “Seventy-three milliseconds. Enough for a syncopation. If the axis notices, it’ll redline again.”

Gideon gave a soft, approving grunt. “Jazz in the cathedral. I’ll take it over the dirge.”

The corridor ahead narrowed until they had to walk single file, boots sinking into the yielding floor like pilgrims in soft snow. Each footfall released a puff of warm, metallic air—iron and ozone, the scent of old surgeries done by candlelight. Overhead, the bioluminescence thinned to a bruised gold, as though the walls themselves were bruising from the inside out. The anomaly thread pulsed once, a soft green flare against Sona’s sternum, then settled into the long, deliberate rhythm of a sleeper who refuses to wake on schedule.

Corv counted it out softly, lips barely moving. “One... two... three-and... four.” The charm against his wrist ticked in counter-time, the failed candidate’s brass warm as breath. “She’s still here,” he said. “Not her body—just the stamp her pulse left on the metal.”

Sona gave a small, private nod. “Pulse is a signature. Paper fades; rhythm doesn’t.” She touched the wall; the cartilage yielded, then resealed without a seam. “It’s learning us,” she added. “Slowly. Like a tongue tasting unfamiliar spice.”

Jian’s scanner chimed—a polite, almost apologetic note. “Pressure differential ahead. Looks like a sphincter valve. If doctrine doesn’t like our tempo, it’ll squeeze us out or digest us.”

Gideon unslung his rifle—not to aim, but to use the composite stock as a tuning fork. He tapped it against the yielding wall; the sound that came back was low, wet, unmistakably muscular. “Peristaltic corridor,” he said. “One way in. No refunds.”

They advanced. The corridor contracted around them, warm and intimate as a throat. The air thickened; each breath tasted of salt and distant storms. Sona’s lungs laboured, not from exertion but from the weight of borrowed time. The anomaly thread flickered once, twice, then steadied—green-gold, unmistakable, a minor third above the axis’s dominant chord.

Corv spoke into the hush, voice pitched low enough that only Sona would feel the vibration. “You can still set it down. Let the axis have its silence.”

Her fingers tightened around nothing, as if crushing invisible spice. “Silence is a luxury. I’m carrying the comma the sentence forgot.” She glanced back at Jian, then Gideon. “We vote with skin, remember? Mine’s already marked.”

The corridor exhaled; they spilled into the antechamber ankle-deep in fluid that looked like liquid pearl but tasted of tears. A low ripple crossed the pool, sourceless. With it came a pressure release—not sound, but the sudden absence of the cortisol hum, as if the chamber had paused mid-catechism to listen.

Sona lifted her bead. The anomaly thread flared, thin as breath yet bright enough to cast shadows. Without filters, it spoke: a single, questioning pulse. Green. Gold. Silence.
