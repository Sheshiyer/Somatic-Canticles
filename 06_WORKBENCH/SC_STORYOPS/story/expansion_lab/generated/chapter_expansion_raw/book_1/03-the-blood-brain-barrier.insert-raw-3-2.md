Corv hooked the silver key from Sona’s palm with two fingers, the way a card-sharper lifts an ace.  
“Consent form, revision four,” he said, sliding it between them like a wager.  
Gideon read the line aloud: *Subject retains right to self-withdraw tissue at any phase.*  
“Too late,” he murmured, “the Klei’s already half-sown.”  
Sona tapped the clause with a lacquered nail. “Clause is retro-active. Pull it out if you want; we’ll suture the breach with saline until you decide.”  
Jian laughed once, short, the sound of a valve cracking. “You want me to aspirate my own paranoia? I’d need a bigger needle.”  
Corv offered the violet membrane like a folded map. “Trade you this for one honest answer: did you come here to save the brain, or to see if it could still bruise you?”  
Sona’s voice dropped to a nurse’s register. “Both. Same as any surgeon who ever flinched.”  
Gideon felt the lattice along his ribs flex, neither threat nor promise, just listening. “If I say stay, what does the protocol demand of you?”  
Sona met his eyes. “That we match your cadence. No faster.”  
Corv snorted. “And people think the Tower is cruel.”  
Jian lifted the canister, lighter now. “Then let’s move before the taste of ginger turns into a lecture.”  
They stepped onto the platform in the order they’d arrived, only this time nobody checked who stood closest to the edge.
