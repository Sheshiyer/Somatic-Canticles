Spine telemetry pulses amber at three-second cadence—sub-clinical warning, nothing critical. Sona flexes her shoulders inside the suit and watches the line draw itself across her HUD: shallow theta, rising cortisol, tidal volume already recovering. The corridor is quiet except for the low, satisfied hiss of the lattice equalizing pressure behind her.  

Aft of her, Keshap’s voice drops into the open channel, stripped of its earlier banter. “Still reading elevated noradrenaline. You’re running a quarter-point above baseline. Want me to walk you through a parasympathetic reset?”  

“Negative,” she says, tasting cedar in the back of her throat like a memory of sap. “I’m cycling it down on the exhale. Keep your bandwidth for the manifold logs.”  

He doesn’t argue; protocol is protocol. The team is down to three breathing bodies, and every watt counts. Somewhere behind the bulkhead, the station’s circulatory pumps shift phase—an audible double heartbeat that syncs with her own slower rhythm. Keshap’s fingers flick across the projected readouts, tagging anomalies in the weave.  

“Field note,” he mutters, almost to himself. “Post-event cortisol spike at 92 µg per deciliter. That’s a match for the delta we saw after the Tycho incident. Cross-ref with the endocrine-muse tables?”  

“Already queued,” Sona answers, though she hasn’t moved her hands. The lattice answers for her: a soft green acknowledgment blooms on the wall beside her shoulder. She exhales once, twice, letting the count stretch—four in, six out, seven in, eight out—until the color leaches from amber to a steady, unremarkable blue.  

Keshap’s footsteps slow, reluctant. “You sure you’re not running silent pain? We’ve got a med-patch pre-charged.”  

She snorts, the sound small but honest. “If I were hurting, you’d hear it in the waveform. I’m not the one who tried to handshake with a dream of mercury.”  

A beat of silence. Then, the smallest laugh—dry, almost embarrassed—crackles through the comm. “Fair.”  

She turns, finally, and finds him standing two arm-lengths back, helmet clipped to his belt, dark curls flattened where the seal ring pressed. The whites of his eyes are still shot through with capillary red, after-image of the manifold flare. He looks less like an archivist than a man who just swam through someone else’s dream and came up coughing starlight.  

“Telemetry says you held pattern integrity at 98.2 percent,” he offers, as if she needs the reassurance.  

Sona shrugs. “The weave wanted to unknot. I just gave it permission.”  

“Next time ask permission out loud,” he says, deadpan. “The safety board prefers audible consent.”  

“Noted.” She glances at the lattice—threads of green light quivering like harp strings under silent wind—then back to him. “You logging the cedar trace?”  

“Captured and compressed. Smells like post-rain desert.” He hesitates, then slips the recorder from his belt and thumbs a short sequence. A miniature spectrogram unfurls between them: a soft fan of frequencies centered on 3.2 kHz, edges feathered with harmonic overtones. “Ghost resonance,” he says. “It’s still clinging to your skin mic.”  

Sona lifts her wrist, half curious. The cedar ghost follows the motion, a shimmer of scent made almost visible. She lowers her hand, and the smell settles, tender as body heat.  

Keshap pockets the recorder. “I’ll file it under ‘non-pathogenic ephemera.’ Unless Medical decides it’s another psychotropic bleed.”  

“They can try,” she says. “But the weave already voted.”  

He exhales through his nose, gaze drifting to the exit hatch. “Field protocol says we debrief in the clean room in fifteen. You good to walk?”  

“I’m walking,” she answers, and takes one experimental step. The corridor is level, lights steady—no residual vertigo. She tracks her footfall on the HUD: pressure even, cadence smooth. A soft ping confirms gait within tolerance.  

Keshap falls in beside her, pace unhurried. For a stretch they move in companionable quiet, the only accompaniment the low susurrus of the lattice breathing with them.  

“Hey,” he says after a moment, voice pitched lower. “That thing you did—letting the manifold borrow your exhale pattern—risky.”  

“Calculated.” Sona keeps her eyes forward. “It wanted a lullaby, not a lecture.”  

He huffs. “Next time you decide to harmonize with a manifold, give me a heads-up. I’d like to record the waveform before Medical decides it’s classified.”
