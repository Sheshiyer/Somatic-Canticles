The mirrored hallway ended sooner than any of them expected, folding into a small chamber whose walls were the matte black of a pupil in bright light. No echo, no reflection—just an absence that drank every footfall. At its center, a single pressure pad glowed the color of capillary blood. Above it, a narrow vent exhaled a scent equal parts antiseptic and campfire ash.  

Sona halted first, the Klei sphere cupped against her sternum like a second heart rehearsing its debut. The warmth had migrated—no longer just the sphere’s heat, but a returned pulse that felt older than the body holding it. She measured the interval with the back of her tongue: still 0.8 Hz, but the gain had doubled. Each cycle rode the hinge of her jaw as though reminding her who truly carried whom.  

“Micro-tear at the sphere’s equator,” Gideon said, voice low enough to qualify as confession. “Stress fracture from the systolic push. Integrity at ninety-one percent and dropping.” He did not look at Sona; he looked at the fracture’s reflection in her pupils instead.  

Corv leaned closer, thumb tracing a ghost circle around the fracture without touching. “Material memory,” he murmured. “The sphere remembers how much force it took to get us here. It’s negotiating amnesty.” He glanced at Jian. “Do we grant it?”  

Jian already had the cuff ribbon open, delta trace scrolling. The carrier wave had begun to wobble—three degrees phase drift, then four. “We grant nothing yet,” he said. “We stabilize. I can rephase the carrier by injecting a controlled arrhythmia across the group. Two beats off, three beats on. Everyone will feel it.”  

Sona’s throat flexed. “Last time you tried controlled arrhythmia in sim, Corv coded for twelve seconds.”  

“Sim didn’t have an exit wound,” Jian replied. He lifted his left hand, showing the underside of the wrist where a small vein stood blue and urgent. “I’ll take the first skipped beat. Anyone else volunteers?”  

Corv flexed his fingers, the obsidian shard pinned between two knuckles. “Give me the skip. I like how silence tastes just before the body remembers it isn’t dead.”  

Gideon caught Sona’s gaze, asking without words. She shook her head once—minute, final. “No skips. We ride the tear.” She turned the sphere so the fracture faced outward, a thin white line catching the vent’s faint red glow. “If the sphere breaks, the heartbeat scatters. Good. Memory should not travel in one intact piece. Too easy to archive, too easy to forget.”  

Jian’s HUD flickered amber. Phase drift five degrees. “Decision window closes in fifteen seconds.”  

Sona closed her free hand over the fracture, feeling the heat sting. “Decision is made. We breach the pad together, no stutter step. When the sphere ruptures, each of us catches what sticks. No pooling, no curation. Raw scatter.”  

Corv’s grin flickered, half mischief, half relief. “Democracy by shrapnel. I can work with that.”  

Gideon adjusted the Klei one last time, thinning it until the sphere appeared to float above Sona’s palm, fracture gleaming like a fault line in moonlight. “On three,” he said, “we drop our weight onto the pad. Sphere cracks against sternum, heartbeat disperses, we keep moving before the Engine drafts a rescue protocol.”  

Jian tapped the ribbon twice, locking the delta trace so the wave could collapse without telemetry. “One.”  

Sona inhaled, tasting salt and iron and the ghost of hospital blankets. “Two.”  

Corv pressed the shard against the sphere’s fracture—not cutting, just marking the exact vector. “Two-point-nine.”  

Gideon exhaled. “Close enough.”  

They stepped.  

The pad gave beneath their combined mass with the soft resistance of living cartilage. The sphere cracked along the scored line, releasing a pulse of silver light that split into four ribbons. Each ribbon struck a sternum, sank under the rib line, and vanished.  

For one suspended heartbeat the chamber had no walls, no vent, no floor—only the afterimage of a communal systole that felt like the entire team inhaling at once.  

Then the chamber snapped back. Lights steadied. The vent exhaled one last breath, this time odorless.  

Sona’s tremor was gone. Gideon’s lag had flattened. Corv’s resting pulse read two beats below baseline—the lowest since induction. Jian’s ribbon showed four green lines, no delta trace left to chase.  

They looked at one another without speaking, the shared silence louder than laughter.  

Corv slipped the obsidian shard into the empty hollow where the sphere had been, a small black seed in place of a heart. “For later,” he said. “In case we forget whose fracture this was.”  

Jian closed the cuff, sealing the vitals ribbon into the permanent log. The amber light winked out.  

Together they turned toward the single exit that had appeared opposite the pad: a plain metal door without handle, sensor plate, or label. It stood ajar by two centimeters, enough to let through a draft that smelled of nothing at all.  

Sona led this time, heartbeat in her chest now entirely her own.
