Keshap taps the side of his visor. A single red glyph flickers—L-3 drift, two percent off-nadir.  
“Tiny,” he mutters, “until it snowballs.”

Sona flicks the glyph away with her glove. “Lattice’s still digesting the pattern. Give it sixty seconds.”

He exhales through teeth. “We don’t have sixty. Ops wants us out of the corridor before the next vesper tide.”

She checks the HUD: tide ETA, 3:47. Enough time for one last calibration sweep.

Keshap fingers the edge of his glove, nerves surfacing as quiet percussion against the cuff. “You ever think the manifold keeps a scrapbook of our breath? Like—favorite exhalations filed under ‘repeat customer’?”

Sona snorts, soft. “If it does, your coffee sigh from hour four is definitely laminated.”

He laughs once—short, surprised—then lets the sound settle into the lattice’s breathing walls. The corridor seems to inhale with them, a slow communal ventricle.

“Point is,” he says, sobering, “next time it asks for a cradle song, maybe hum instead of handing over the whole score.”

She tilts her head. “Humming’s just breath without intent. Intent is the anchor. You know that.”

“Yeah. I also know intent can be subpoenaed by any system with a long enough memory.” His voice drops. “Crowley’s Law, paraphrased.”

The lattice answers with a low harmonic, not quite agreement, not quite warning. Sona feels it vibrate in the bones behind her sternum—an echo wearing her own pulse.

She closes her eyes, counts four beats, releases. Cedar and ozone leave her tongue together. “Protocol tweak,” she says, already typing into wristglass. “We cap the loan at three respiratory cycles. Fourth cycle auto-rescinds, no handshake required.”

Keshap watches the new rule scroll green. “That’ll shave the risk curve to a tenth. Nice.”

He offers a fist. She taps it once, polymer on polymer, sealing the compromise.

The corridor lights dim to amber: ninety-second mark. No panic, just a nudge.

They turn shoulder to shoulder, footfalls syncing unspoken. The lattice exhales around them, warmer now, tasting faintly of yesterday’s cinnamon and today’s cedar—memories stirred but not kept.
