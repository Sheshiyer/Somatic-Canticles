“Is that thyme I smell, or the ghost of a cafeteria?” Jian mutters, squinting at the lichen as if it might answer.  
Sona snorts, the sound half-curved in amusement, half-grooved by fatigue. “Scents lose their CV around here. Everything becomes autobiography.”  
She feels for the zipper on her wrist-pouch, slow, deliberate—buying clock. The migraine paper-cuts across Jian’s optic nerve again; he swallows, counts the stutter of his pulse: seven this minute, eight next. Not numbers but liabilities.

He lifts the field recorder, thumb hovering. “If we log the threshold before we cross we can still bill it as recon, not trespass. Buy ourselves a grace note.”  
Sona’s smile compresses to the thin edge of a boning knife. “Grace notes are pre-indictment, Li. You know the ledger.”  
The corridor feels shorter than the map promised, as though the rock has been breathing in. One wall shows a vein of garnet that wasn’t there when they started. Garnet for constancy, garnet for unasked oaths. Sona’s gloved finger traces it, not reverent, just measuring.

Jian flips the recorder open; the screen coughs up a single red pixel. Low battery, or low courage. He thumbs the mute toggle anyway. Protocol says they still have to speak their consent into the record, but protocol also says if the patient flatlines while you hesitate, the silence is admissible.  
“Give me a question worth answering,” he says, “and I’ll roll.”  
Sona’s eyes stay forward, but her voice folds around him like sheet-music left on a radiator. “What happens if the signal outruns the transduction?”  
He laughs once, brittle. “Same thing that happens when the punchline beats the joke: somebody leaves the room bleeding.”

The garnet flickers, or they do; impossible to tell. Jian feels the benzoin surge again, resinous, medicinal, a grandmother’s perfume turned forensics. “Taste that?” he asks.  
Sona nods. “It’s the chair bleeding apology into the air. Old leather remembers the last spine it broke.”  
A pressure-release valve clicks inside Jian’s skull—audible to both of them, though neither speaks it. The migraine unclenches, not gone, just turning its face away.

He squares his shoulders toward the narrowing slit. “I’ll go first, you audit.”  
Sona tilts her head, bird-curious. “Since when do martyrs ask permission?”  
“Since the insurance rider started covering survivor’s guilt but not manslaughter.”  
She exhales, the small huff of someone conceding a chess piece she’d already planned to sacrifice. “Fine. But you owe me the next hallucination. I pick the soundtrack.”

They synchronize watches—an antique courtesy, redundant once their glands start keeping better time than quartz. Jian sets the recorder to continuous, no loop-back; if they die, the file will live long enough to accuse.  
Sona lifts her left hand, two fingers split in the gesture Crowley called the sign of the enterer. Jian answers with the sign of silence, thumb across throat, but gentler, almost apologetic. A promise, or a postponement.

The corridor seems to wait, listening the way a confessor waits for the first honest verb.  
Jian tastes benzoin again, thinner now, like a penitent’s incense burning itself out. He lifts one foot, sets it down. The stone feels softer, as if it expects him. Behind them the chair exhales again: *I have carried what I can.*  
Sona follows, shoulder brushing his, no ceremony, just the small pressure of a hand that says *don’t lock your knees* and *I’m still here*.
