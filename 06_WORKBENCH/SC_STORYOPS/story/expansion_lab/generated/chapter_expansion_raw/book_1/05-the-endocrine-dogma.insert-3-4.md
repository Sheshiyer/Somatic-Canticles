Jian’s voice cracked the hush first, pitched low to spare the walls.  
“Fine. Sona, you’ve got the bead, but the protocol says we flag anything above nine microcuries. That pulse was seventeen. We log, then we decide.”

Sona gave a single nod, the motion economical, as if saving torque for later.  
“Already logged. The log is screaming. Decision’s the part we’re short on.”

Gideon shifted his weight, boots whispering across the floor’s thin film.  
“Screaming’s relative. What I hear is the endocrine dogma reminding us we still have livers.”

He meant it as levity, but the joke landed like soft tissue against a scalpel. Sona exhaled through her teeth.  
“Crowley would call this the Abyssal Lullaby. You hum it just before you step off the map.”

Corv, who had been watching the pool with the patience of someone used to waiting for sentient water to blink, finally spoke.  
“Map’s already off the table. We’re in the margin where footnotes breed.”

The liquid pearl rippled again; this time the motion carried an after-image, a faint green-gold echo that lingered on retinas like the memory of a migraine halo. Sona thumbed the bead’s ridge, feeling the barely-there vibration that said the anomaly was still speaking, just slower now, syllables dragged through viscous time.  
“It wants a name,” she muttered. “Names are binding. Binding is leverage. Leverage is risk.”

Jian lifted the scanner, then let it fall back against his thigh.  
“Risk is already priced in. Question is who pays the premium.”

He looked at Gideon, who responded with the tiniest lift of one shoulder, half apology, half dare.  
“My premium’s paid in scar tissue. I can spot us one more layer.”

Sona’s fingers brushed the faint lattice of burns along her forearm, souvenirs from the last time they’d misread a threshold.  
“And if the layer’s already grafted into the architecture?” she asked. “If the comma she forgot is actually a ligature?”

Corv inhaled, the sound deliberate, almost ceremonial.  
“Then we negotiate with the ligature. We ask it what kind of sentence it wants to finish.”

A silence pooled, thicker than the liquid underfoot. The cortisol hum had not restarted; instead, the absence felt like air after a slammed door. Somewhere in the distance, a valve ticked—once, twice—then settled back into dormancy.

Jian took a cautious step closer to Sona, close enough to see the individual freckles standing out on the bridge of her nose, close enough for her to smell the faint ozone of the bead’s power cell.  
“Consent isn’t a solo signature,” he said quietly. “We all have to countersign.”

She met his eyes, pupils blown wide to drink the low light.  
“I know. That’s why my skin’s already marked. I’m not asking any of you to bleed for me.”

Gideon snorted, the sound sharp enough to cut the tension for half a second.  
“Darling, we’re way past asking. We’re at the part where the body writes IOUs in capillary script.”

He dipped two fingers into the pool, lifted them. The liquid clung, opalescent, then slid off in perfect droplets that hit the floor with soft taps like muted metronome beats.  
“Pearl my ass. This is diluted regret—forty-proof nostalgia and twelve-proof terror. No wonder the bead’s chatty.”

Corv crouched, fingertips hovering just above the surface.  
“Nostalgia can be weaponized. Terror can be tuned. Either way, we’re out of sterile gloves.”

Sona felt the bead twitch again, a subtle jerk as if it had caught the scent of a new frequency. She lowered her hand until the bead rested just above the pool, close enough for the liquid to lap at the ceramic shell like a curious tongue.  
“If we give it a name, it gets a vote,” she warned. “No half-measures.”

Jian’s mouth twisted into something between grin and grimace.  
“Names are half-measures. Full measures are what happens after the name sticks.”

He glanced at the scanner’s dark screen, then at the corridor behind them, empty except for their own footprints already softening at the edges as the floor reabsorbed them.  
“Protocol also says we don’t leave an open anomaly behind us. Either we resolve it or we carry it. Carrying costs scale with distance.”

Sona’s eyes flicked to Corv, who was still crouched, motionless, as if waiting for the floor to confess.  
“Corv, you still have the enneagram overlay loaded?”

He nodded once, slow.  
“Seven of Cups, The Tower reversed, and a whisper of Four’s melancholy. Overlay says: attachment wound dressed as curiosity.”

Gideon barked a laugh that echoed off the vaulted ceiling.  
“Curiosity killed the cat, but satisfaction brought it back—missing a few organs, maybe, but definitely back.”

Sona let the bead sink a millimeter lower; the liquid parted around it with a sigh that tasted of iron and linden blossom.  
“Four’s melancholy. That’s mine. I’ll carry that share. You three pick your own.”

Jian’s jaw tightened.  
“That’s not how shares work.”

“It is tonight,” she said, tone flat as obsidian. “My mark, my majority stake.”

Corv straightened, knees popping softly.  
“Fine. But we set a boundary. The boundary is silence. No more language leaks until we’re past the antechamber.”

Gideon raised an eyebrow.  
“Silence is a luxury. She just said so.”

“Silence is a treaty,” Corv corrected. “Luxury is what you pay to keep the treaty intact.”

Sona looked at each of them in turn, the bead warming against her palm as if registering the heat of their collective pulse.  
“Treaty accepted. Silence starts when I close my fist. After that, we move single file, no scans, no conversation. We walk the line the comma forgot.”

Jian hesitated, then reached into his pocket and pulled out a thin strip of black silk—the same kind they used to tie off nerve clusters during field dissections. He looped it once around his wrist, tight enough to bite.  
“Collateral,” he said simply. “If I talk, I lose circulation. Fair warning.”

Gideon watched the silk compress skin, then mirrored the gesture with the drawstring of his hood, knotting it around his forearm like a tourniquet of intent.  
“Tourniquet of intent,” he echoed aloud, then winced. “Sorry. Bad habit.”

Corv said nothing. Instead, he drew a single fingertip across the inside of his own wrist, splitting the skin just enough for a bead of blood to well up, dark and glossy under the bioluminescent veins of the ceiling. He touched the bead to the pool; the liquid recoiled, then surged back, absorbing the drop with a soft click, as if a microscopic lock had just turned.

Sona watched the red diffuse, a single thread spiraling out like a fuse.  
“Threshold acknowledged,” she whispered.

She closed her fist around the bead. The green-gold pulse flared once, then stilled. Silence fell—not absence, but deliberate, compacted silence, heavy with withheld exhalations.

They arranged themselves without discussion: Sona at point, bead held chest-high; Corv two steps back, eyes scanning the walls for tremors that might be language trying to leak through; Jian in the middle, scanner powered down but thumb still resting on the activation stud out of reflex; Gideon at rear, hand brushing the small of Sona’s back every third step, the contact brief and grounding, a wordless reassurance that the boundary held.

The fluid underfoot resisted motion just enough to remind them it was alive, a passive participant waiting for one of them to misstep. Each footfall released a faint shimmer, a ripple that traveled ahead of them like a herald too polite to speak. The corridor narrowed, then widened, then narrowed again, its geometry fluid in the way dreams redraw themselves when unobserved. Sona counted heartbeats—hers first, then the quieter, syncopated rhythm that might have been the bead’s, or might have been the corridor’s own pulse. Forty-two beats later, the air changed: cooler, edged with the mineral scent of deep earth after rain.

Behind her, Jian’s thumb twitched against the scanner’s stud; the device stayed dark, but the gesture spoke volumes. She felt it like static against her spine. Ahead, the antechamber’s archway appeared, a soft ellipse that glowed not with light but with the suggestion of light, the memory of illumination after the bulb has burned out. The pool began to slope upward, ankle-deep, then calf-deep, the liquid pearling against their legs like curious kittens.

At the threshold she paused, fist still closed, bead warm against her sternum. She could feel the others’ breaths brushing her neck, warm, irregular, human. The silence stretched, elastic but not yet fraying. She lifted the bead to her lips, kissed the ceramic once—dry, quick, a promise or an apology—then opened her hand.

Green. Gold. Silence.
