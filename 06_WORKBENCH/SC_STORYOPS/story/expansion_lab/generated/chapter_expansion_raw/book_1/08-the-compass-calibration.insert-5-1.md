Sona rubs the bridge of her nose, the carbon-fiber stylus still warm from Jian’s last toggle.  
“Three minutes,” she mutters. “That’s shorter than the half-life of my caffeine buzz.”

Corv lifts the chronograph like it’s a live grenade. “Plenty of time for regret if we mess it up.”

The droplet on Jian’s cuff trembles, catching the overhead strip as a tiny convex moon.  
“Biology says it’ll bead for forty-seven more seconds,” he says, “then gravity wins. Gives us a window to test the micro-drift against droplet descent. Cheap oracle.”

Sona snorts. “You’re turning condensing sweat into a spirit level?”  
“Cheaper than a theodolite and twice as honest.”

Corv thumbs the stud; the chronograph ticks into the pocket, silencing itself like a bribed referee.  
“Clock’s running. If the bearing wobbles, we yank him out at the first 0.18 hiccup, no argument.”

Jian tilts his wrist, watching the droplet elongate. “Philosophy question: is a bead of failure still failure if nobody sees it fall?”

“Only if the Compass logs it,” Sona deadpans. “Otherwise it’s just bad poetry.”
