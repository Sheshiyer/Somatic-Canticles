Gideon slid the journal deeper into his coat. The cracked spine pressed a ridge against his rib, a quiet second heartbeat. Sona’s inhale stuttered on something too thin to be laughter. She tasted rust, ozone, and the almost-sweet after-image of cardamom that had appeared on her tongue the moment the lattice finalised the bearing. “We’re leaking flavour,” she said. “The calibration is digesting us in reverse.”

Jian’s cuff pinged once: the anomaly glyph, amber, polite. He muted it, but not before the bearing slid a quiet vector into the same corner of his retina—0.3 degrees port of its last mark. A drift so small it could be heartbeat error, yet he felt the lattice tighten around it like a muscle learning a new posture.

Gideon patted the pocket containing the journal. “Four hundred grams of deferred testimony,” he said. “We carry it until the city decides whether confession or combustion is cheaper tonight.”

Sona lifted her hand, palm parallel to the floor as if she were testing rain that hadn’t arrived yet. “The corridor is thinning,” she observed. “Listen.”

They all heard it: the walls drawing back a fraction of a centimetre, a polite concession that space itself could now be negotiated. A faint vibration—somewhere between purr and shiver—passed through the soles of their boots. Corv’s pupils widened, dark mirrors catching nothing but the idea of distance. “It’s letting us leave the way you let a bird out the window,” he said. “No goodbye, just the air changing ownership.”

Sona drew a slow breath through her teeth, tasting both forks at once. “Memory already chose the warm tongue,” she said. “The glacier can keep its secrets for now.”

Jian adjusted the bearing by the same 0.3 degrees, felt the change register as a soft unlocking between shoulder blades. The route held—no longer a command, simply an address that hadn’t yet been delivered.

Behind them, the corridor exhaled one last metallic sigh, the sound a compass makes when it forgives the world for moving without permission.

They walked toward the rectangle of night air without looking back.
