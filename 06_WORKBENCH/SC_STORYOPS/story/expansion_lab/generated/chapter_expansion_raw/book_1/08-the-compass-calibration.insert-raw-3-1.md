Corv flips the salvaged chronograph open once more, studying the stilled heartbeat like a dealer who’s already lost the bet.  
“Three short, two long,” he murmurs, “not Morse, just panic trying to spell its own name.”  
Sona tilts her head, hearing the same rhythm echoed in the coolant pulse overhead.  
“Could’ve been worse,” she offers. “Could’ve been mine.”  

Jian exhales, the first full lungful since the corridor narrowed.  
“Next time we end the rehearsal before the subject flat-lines. That’s not a calibration, that’s a vivisection.”  
There’s no accusation in it, only the dry settle of protocol regret.  

Sona thumbs the safety off her own console, lets the soft click answer for consent.  
“I already logged the drift. The Compass skews east when cortisol spikes past 200 ng/ml. We dial the gain down by 0.3 and the map holds.”  
Corv nods, pocketing the chronograph at last.  
“Translation: don’t scare the oracle. Noted.”  

A brief hush follows—no visionary bloom, just the three of them breathing at slightly different rates, like asynchronous metronomes finding a shared bar.  
The corridor adjusts its pitch again, a polite mechanical sigh, as if embarrassed by its earlier tantrum.  
Coolant drips from Jian’s cuff, hesitates, then chooses the floor over his boot.  
Sona watches it fall, laughs once—small, surprised, the sound of a body remembering it still owns lungs.  

Corv tests the new bearing on his wrist slate, sees the route pulse steady.  
“Ninety-eight point two,” he confirms.  
Jian snorts. “Point two is vanity. Ninety-eight is mercy.”  
Corv shrugs. “I’ll take the vanity. Keeps the math honest.”
