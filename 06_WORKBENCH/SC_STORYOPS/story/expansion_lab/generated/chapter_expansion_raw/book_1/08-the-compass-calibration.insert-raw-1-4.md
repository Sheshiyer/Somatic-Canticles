The corridor exhales a metallic sigh, softer than a page turning, louder than a memory.  
Jian feels it first along the inside of his left ankle—a cool ribbon of air threading the gap between liner and boot leather, carrying the taste of old solder and night-time concrete. He registers the data the way he registers pain: automatically, intimately. Sona’s heartbeat flashes in the lower quadrant of his visor—82 bpm, steady, the quiet tempo of someone who has already forgiven the world for being late.

Behind them the failed candidate’s shadow is still flat against the wall, no thicker than the grease pencil he used to scrawl *The glacier always wins.* The letters have begun to flake, raining graphite like winter pollen. Somewhere inside the ventilation grid, a pump cycles on, cycles off, indifferent.

Sona tilts her head, listening. “We’re leaking flavour,” she says, voice hushed as if the air itself might overhear. “Cardamom and rust. The calibration is digesting us in reverse.”

Jian rolls the new bearing in his inner field—0.3 degrees west of the last mark, cobalt caret hovering like a polite question. He answers it with a soft click of tongue against comm tooth, no words, just the subvocal equivalent of *still here.* The lattice answers with a bloom of green: *acknowledged.*

Corv watches from the corner of the corridor, weight balanced on the balls of his feet, the way a dancer waits for the downbeat he already owns. His pupils are wide, dark mirrors catching nothing but the idea of distance. “The route wants a witness,” he murmurs. “Not a verdict.”

Gideon slips the failed candidate’s journal into an outer pocket, cracked spine pressing a ridge against his ribs like a second heartbeat. Four hundred grams of panic, cured in time. He does not open it; some stories are best read only after the reader has left the building where they were written.

They stand in a loose ring, four bodies adjusting to the same tempo as the corridor begins to unhouse them. The walls narrow, then widen, politely, as though architecture itself has decided to loosen its belt after the long fast of calibration. A fine mist rises from the seams where floor meets wall—condensation, not vapour, tasting of printer ink and human iron. Sona wipes a bead from her upper lip, flicks it toward Jian. He catches it one-handed, holds the tiny crimson sphere to the cuff scanner.

“Signature matches the hemoglobin tracer,” he says. “We’re still inside our own capillary map.”

“Good,” she answers. “I hate borrowing circulation.”

The quip is automatic, brittle, the kind of humour meant to hold the moment together until something stronger sets. Jian counts thirteen steps before the pressure valve ticks. A bead of coolant hisses from his lattice cuff, spirals upward, clings to the ceiling like a miniature moon. It hangs for a heartbeat, then bursts—soft pop of distant applause.

“System relief,” Sona says. “The bearing’s no longer pressurised in silicon; it’s equalising through us.”

“Biology wins the staring contest,” Gideon mutters. “Always does.”

Ahead, the corridor forks. From the left mouth comes the cold exhalation of a glacier that has never met a calendar. From the right drifts the warmer breath of diesel and wet asphalt, the smell of cities that keep their lights on so the night never has to admit it’s afraid. Sona inhales both drafts, one nostril each, exhales through her teeth to mix them. Memory already cast the vote. Consensus is just choreography.

Jian adjusts the bearing by the same 0.3 degrees, feels the change register as a soft unlocking between shoulder blades. The route holds—no longer a command, simply an address that hasn’t yet been delivered. Behind them, the corridor exhales a final click—door sealing, history closing its own cover. No one turns. Turning would imply doubt, and doubt has already been metabolised into the trail behind them.

They walk toward the rectangle of night air without looking back, four silhouettes carrying the new compass in the only place it can survive: the difference between one another’s steps.
