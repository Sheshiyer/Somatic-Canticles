Jian exhales through his teeth, the sound thin and sharp.  
“Clock’s at minus nine.”

Sona doesn’t answer; she’s watching the last bar on her wristband crawl from violet to dead gray. One hour since the last delta spike, fifteen minutes since the Compass red-lined. She thumbs the solder patch on her suit cuff where the bio-gel bled out earlier. It’s stopped leaking, but the fabric smells like burnt copper and salty skin.

Gideon leans into the wall, palm splayed, testing how much give the alloy still has. “Feels like lungs,” he mutters. “Keeps trying to collapse.”

Jian snorts. “Good. Means the corridor still thinks we’re alive.”

A single drip lands between them—condensed coolant, maybe. It splashes on Gideon’s boot, darkening the leather. He toes it aside, smearing the droplet into the pattern already etched on the floor: concentric circles, nine and a half of them, scratched with a scalpel sometime before any of them arrived. Sona crouches, runs her gloved fingertip along the broken ring.

“Failed candidate left a signature,” she says. “Half a loop short. Classic Eight fear of completion.”

“Yeah,” Jian answers, voice flat. “Crowley would say they choked at Da’ath.”

Gideon rolls his shoulder again, harder. The grafted rib clicks; he winces. “Not Crowley I’m worried about. It’s the other nine still stuck inside the walls.”

Sona straightens, flexes her fingers until the knuckles pop. “We still have the choice. Abort to surface, get a full reset.”

“No reset,” Gideon says. “Reset means another week of quarantine. Reset means they swap in another hopeful.” He taps the wall with the side of his fist, soft, like knocking on a coffin lid. “I’m not feeding anyone else to this corridor.”

Jian swipes the Compass interface, flicks three toggles, then a fourth hidden under a strip of tape. The amber line stutters, flares gold, settles back to amber. “Route’s stable for twenty-three minutes. After that, drift factor spikes again.”

Gideon nods once. “So we recalibrate on the fly.”

“On the fly,” Sona repeats, tasting the words. “You ever try to re-tune an adrenal gland while sprinting?”

“Couple times,” Jian answers, without looking up. “Always end up spitting blood.”

Gideon gives a short, surprised laugh that dies almost immediately. “Look. We pull the gain on the cortisol receptors down two notches, boost oxytocin baseline just enough to mute panic. Keeps the Compass from pinging our fear as a false vector.”

“Risky,” Sona says. “Too much oxy and we go soft. Soft means slow. Slow means walls close, game over.”

“Not doing nothing,” Gideon counters. “Soft heart beats longer than no heart.”

Jian taps the side of his boot, thinking. The sound is soft, deliberate, like practicing Morse. He glances at the failed candidate’s scratched circles one last time. “Option two: we borrow their failure. Break the last half-loop ourselves, but do it clean, finish the glyph. Turn the flaw into a key.”

Sona tilts her head. “Door opens, we walk through the exit wound.”

“Exactly.”

Gideon sucks air through his teeth. “Leaves a scar the corridor remembers.”

“Scars are maps,” Jian says. He holds out the Compass; its amber line wavers, then lengthens by a hair. “Clock’s minus seven. Decide.”

Sona looks first at Gideon, then at Jian. Her pupils are wide, black moons. “Fine. We do it your way, but if anyone starts quoting Liber AL halfway, I’m punching them in the throat.”

Jian almost smiles. “Deal.”

They move in quiet symmetry. Gideon pulls the micro-injector from his belt, dials the ratio with the tip of his tongue between his teeth. Sona unsheathes the scalpel she swore she’d never use, kneels, and completes the final half-loop in one fluid stroke. Metal on metal, a spark, the smell of ozone. Jian watches the Compass glyph settle into a thin, steady line—no tremor, no flicker.

Above them, the corridor exhales like it’s been holding its breath since the last team died. The temperature drops a single degree; condensation beads on Gideon’s lashes. He blinks it away.

“Route’s stable,” Jian says, quieter now. “Ready to move.”
